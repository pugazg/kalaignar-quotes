## KQ-CCM-0478

- Canonical Tamil: [`0478.md`](../../../quotes/0478.md)
- Source status: `verified_from_scan`

> The Revolutionary Poet sang, “Let us spread the principle of common ownership to all eight directions; let us guard *punithamodathai* as our very life”! Today, has not “revolution” become the act of laying out a bed for the bribing blandishments of private ownership! The future will spit on those who continue to lie with their eyes closed!

**Translation note:** The high-resolution audit confirms the source-visible poetic forms `புனிதமோடதை` and `கொஞ்சலுக்கு`. The semantically irregular `புனிதமோடதை` is retained as **punithamodathai** rather than silently normalized from another version of the poem; the surrounding proposition is translated only from the canonical text.

## KQ-CCM-0484

- Canonical Tamil: [`0484.md`](../../../quotes/0484.md)
- Source status: `verified_from_scan`

> We split a mountain into stones. From one stone we make an idol. The other stones become halls. They become towers. They become enclosing walls. Not every stone can become an idol. If they all did, there could be no temple. The stone that is the idol must not forget that it too came from the mountain just like the other stones. Only because the temple exists does the idol receive *abhishekam* and worship! Otherwise, under which tree or at which bathing steps would it lie? This is a truth that everyone holding every responsibility in an organization must understand!

**Translation note:** `அபிஷேகம்` is retained as **abhishekam**; no outside ritual explanation is added because the source analogy itself supplies the required sense.

## KQ-CCM-0496

- Canonical Tamil: [`0496.md`](../../../quotes/0496.md)
- Source status: `verified_from_scan`

> There is no separate substance called “amirtham.” The Revolutionary Poet explains that all exalted things are called amirtham to signify excellence therefore Valluvar calls the rain that comes from the sky to sustain the world amirtham

**Translation note:** The canonical scan explicitly has no inserted punctuation between `புரட்சிக் கவிஞர்` and `அதனால்தான்`, and no terminal punctuation. The English deliberately preserves that source-visible punctuation anomaly rather than silently repairing it. `அமிழ்தம்` is retained as **amirtham** because the quote itself is defining the term.
